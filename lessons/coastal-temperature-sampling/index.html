Comparing Data Carefully with Weekly Averages and Consistent Patterns

Academic English · B2 (estimated)

Learners will interpret weekly averages and consistent with in the reading context and use them accurately in new situations.

Open exercises in HọcTA

Reading

A marine science class is comparing water temperatures at two coastal bays during the spring. One buoy records a reading every fifteen minutes, while the other sends data once an hour. Before calculating weekly averages, the analyst will align each time window so that the same part of the day is represented at both sites. The team will check sensor notes for gaps and flag days affected by maintenance. A difference between two averages should not be treated as a trend on its own. The researchers will describe a pattern as consistent with local weather only when it appears in several readings and agrees with the station log. They will publish the method with the data so another class can repeat the comparison.
